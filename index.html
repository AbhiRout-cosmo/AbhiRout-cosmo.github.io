Chern–Simons modified gravity

Parity-violating extensions of general relativity and what they predict for compact objects and cosmology.

Explain

General relativity treats left and right the same. Chern–Simons gravity adds a scalar field ϑ coupled to the Pontryagin density, a curvature term that changes sign under a mirror reflection. Wherever ϑ varies, spacetime responds differently to left- and right-handed disturbances.

S = ∫ d4x √−g [ κR + α4 ϑ *RR − β2 (∇ϑ)2 − V(ϑ) ]

Black hole perturbation theory

Axial perturbations, quasinormal modes and isospectrality breaking of charged and rotating black holes in dynamical Chern–Simons gravity.

Explain

A disturbed black hole rings down at a set of complex frequencies, its quasinormal modes. Each perturbation obeys a wave equation with an effective potential. In general relativity the axial and polar sectors ring at identical frequencies. Parity violation can break that degeneracy, which gives a clean observational test.

d2Ψdr*2 + [ ω2 − V(r) ] Ψ = 0

Master equation; for Schwarzschild axial modes, V = (1 − 2M/r)[ℓ(ℓ+1)/r2 − 6M/r3].

QFT in curved spacetime

Bound states and particle production in time-dependent backgrounds, and parity-violating greybody factors for black hole emission.

Explain

A time-dependent background mixes positive- and negative-frequency modes, so a state that starts as the vacuum ends up containing particles. The Bogoliubov coefficients measure that mixing. Oscillating soliton-like "breathers" are a controlled setting where both bound states and particle creation can be worked out.

ukout = αk ukin + βk ukin *, ⟨Nk⟩ = |βk|2
hL ≠ hR

Gravitational-wave phenomenology

Observable signatures of parity violation: birefringence of primordial gravitational waves and parity-violating strain from pulsar glitches.

Explain

If gravity has a preferred handedness, the two circular polarizations of a gravitational wave propagate differently. They can differ in amplitude (amplitude birefringence) or in speed (velocity birefringence), and both leave imprints that detectors and CMB polarization can look for.

hA″ + (2 + νA) ℋ hA′ + (1 + μA) k2 hA = 0

A standard parametrization for each helicity A ∈ {R, L}: νR ≠ νL gives amplitude birefringence, μR ≠ μL gives velocity birefringence.

Interactive

Breaking isospectrality

In general relativity, the axial and polar perturbations of a black hole share one spectrum of ringing frequencies. A parity-violating coupling acts on the axial sector, so as it grows the axial modes drift away from their polar partners. Measuring that split in a ringdown signal would be a direct test of parity in gravity.

The anchor points are the Schwarzschild ℓ = 2 overtones n = 0, 1, 2 in units of M. The drift is illustrative, not a computed result.
